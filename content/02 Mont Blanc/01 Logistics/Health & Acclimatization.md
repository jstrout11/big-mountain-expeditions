# Health & Acclimatization — Mont Blanc

## Altitude profile

| Stop | Elevation |
|---|---:|
| Denver baseline | 1,609 m / 5,280 ft |
| Chamonix | 1,035 m / 3,396 ft |
| Plan de l'Aiguille | 2,317 m / 7,602 ft |
| Aiguille du Midi | 3,777 m / 12,392 ft |
| Refuge des Cosmiques (sleep) | 3,613 m / 11,854 ft |
| Mont Blanc du Tacul | 4,248 m / 13,938 ft |
| Mont Maudit summit | 4,468 m / 14,659 ft |
| **Mont Blanc summit** | **4,808 m / 15,777 ft** |

## Acclimatization strategy

**Borderline** — 4,808 m without prior altitude is genuinely high. Plan adequate acclimatization.

### Best — 5–6 day plan
- Day 1: arrive Chamonix, rest
- Day 2: Aiguille du Tour (3,540 m) — sleep at Refuge Albert Premier (2,706 m)
- Day 3: descent, rest in Chamonix
- Day 4: Cosmiques Arête + sleep Cosmiques (3,613 m)
- Day 5: Trois Monts summit

### Standard — 4 day plan
- Day 1: arrive Chamonix
- Day 2: Plan de l'Aiguille → Petit Aiguille Verte day climb (3,512 m)
- Day 3: Cosmiques
- Day 4: summit

### Bare minimum — 3 day plan (NOT RECOMMENDED unless arriving pre-acclimatized)
- Day 1: arrive Chamonix
- Day 2: Cosmiques (sleep at 3,613 m, very compressed)
- Day 3: summit

### Pre-trip
- Ideally arrive Chamonix from another high mountain trip (e.g., Italian/Swiss Alps Aconcagua, etc.) to skip the acclimatization week
- From Denver baseline, the Front Range 14ers in the month before help meaningfully

## Expected symptoms

- **AMS at Cosmiques** is common (40%+ of first-time visitors) — sleep poorly, mild headache
- **AMS at summit ridge** is common even with acclimatization — nausea, fatigue, slow movement
- **HAPE / HACE** rare with this elevation profile, but possible — see warning signs below

## Medications to carry

- **Acetazolamide (Diamox) 125 mg twice daily** — optional prophylactic, start 24h before crossing 2,500 m
- **Dexamethasone 4 mg** × 6 tabs — HACE emergency (doctor-prescribed)
- **Ibuprofen / acetaminophen** — headache, sore muscles
- **Imodium** — just in case
- **Sunscreen mineral SPF 50** — glacier UV brutal
- **Lip balm with SPF**
- **Eye drops** — dry alpine air
- Personal Rx + extras + photos of labels

## Sun protection

- **Glacier glasses, Cat 4 lenses, side shields** — non-negotiable on glacier. Cheap polarized sunglasses are NOT enough.
- **Backup pair** — losing primary at Cosmiques is a trip-ender
- **Goggles** — nice-to-have for storms/wind

## Hydration

- 3–4 L on summit day
- Refuge has potable water; bring 2 Nalgenes
- Hydrate aggressively at Cosmiques — refuge serves drinks, tea

## Warning signs to descend

| Symptom | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mild headache, mild fatigue | AMS | Stop ascent, hydrate, rest |
| Persistent moderate headache, vomiting | Moderate AMS | Diamox, descend if not improving |
| **Ataxia (can't walk a straight line)** | **HACE** | **Descend, dex 8 mg, descend below 3,000 m** |
| **Wet cough, frothy sputum, breathlessness at rest** | **HAPE** | **Descend, nifedipine 30 mg ER** |
| Cyanosis (blue lips/fingertips) | Severe | Descend + medication + emergency |

## Pre-trip athletic considerations

- See [[Training & Skills]] for the lead-in training plan
- Sleep well the week before
- No new supplements or diet changes
- Test Diamox response on a Front Range 14er before trip
