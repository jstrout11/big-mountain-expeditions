# Training & Skills — Mont Blanc

## Demand profile

- **Summit day:** 10–14 hr push, ~4,000 ft gain on summit push, at 4,800 m
- **Technical:** steep snow (Maudit slope 45–55°), glacier travel, crevasse rescue, fixed-line use
- **Altitude:** 4,808 m without long acclimatization profile
- **Cold:** –15 to –25 °C summit ridge typical

`mont_blanc_training_lead_time_months:: 4-6`

## Lead time

**4–6 months** of focused training on top of base fitness. Ideally do Grand Teton ~6 weeks before as a tune-up — see [[01 Grand Teton/index|Grand Teton OS]].

## Specific training

- **Long hikes 6–10 hr with pack**, simulating alpine start (3 a.m. wake)
- **Glacier skills + crevasse rescue refresh** (drill 2× in the 3 months before)
- **Front Range 14ers** — at least 3 in the month before, with one at 14k+ overnight if possible
- **Front-pointing practice on steep snow** (any Colorado 14er couloir in spring: Holy Cross, Quandary, Castle)
- 1–2 rock-climbing days a week at the gym to maintain hand strength + harness familiarity

## Sample weekly structure (Build phase, ~3 months out)

| Day | Workout |
|---|---|
| Mon | Strength 1: heavy lifts (squat, deadlift, press, pull-ups) — 45 min |
| Tue | Run 60–90 min easy + mobility |
| Wed | Strength 2: single-leg, core, stabilizer — 45 min |
| Thu | Run intervals or hills 45–60 min |
| Fri | Recovery / rest |
| Sat | **LONG DAY** — hike with pack 6–10 hr, 3,000–5,000 ft gain |
| Sun | Run easy 60 min OR rest |

## Strength essentials

- **Back squat** — work to 1.5× body weight for 5 reps
- **Deadlift** — work to 1.75× body weight for 5 reps
- **Bulgarian split squat** — 30 lb each hand for 8 reps each leg
- **Step-ups** — 24" box, 30 lb each hand, 8 reps each leg
- **Pull-ups** — 10 strict for body weight

## Cardio benchmarks

- **Vertical kilometer test:** climb 1,000 m of elevation gain — sub-1:00 strong, sub-1:15 reasonable
- **Loaded vertical:** 1,000 m gain with 30 lb pack — target sub-1:45
- **Heart-rate-controlled climb:** climb 3,000 ft with HR capped at 75% max — should feel sustainable for 6+ hours

## Technical skill checklist (drilled within 90 days of trip)

- [ ] **Glacier travel:** rope spacing, butterfly knots, tension management — drilled with partner
- [ ] **Crevasse rescue 3:1**: set up from arrest in <5 min, drilled with rope partner
- [ ] **Self-arrest:** drilled in 4 positions on real snow within 30 days
- [ ] **Steep snow front-pointing:** comfortable on 45°+ snow with crampons + axe
- [ ] **Snow anchor:** picket vertical + deadman, T-slot buried
- [ ] **Anchor building (rock):** 2-piece equalized anchor in <3 min
- [ ] **Rappel + prussik backup:** smooth on single + double rope
- [ ] **Knot review:** figure-8 follow-through, butterfly, prussik, Bachmann, clove, Munter

## Where to drill

### Front Range (Colorado) practice locations
- **St Mary's Glacier (45 min from Denver)** — small but real glacier surface for drills
- **Holy Cross, Quandary, Castle 14ers in spring** — steep snow couloirs
- **Local climbing gym** — anchor building, multi-pitch transitions, rappel practice
- **Backyard / garage** — fixed-rope ascension setup, prussik knot practice

### Pre-trip course options
- **Colorado Mountain School** — multi-day glacier travel + crevasse rescue
- **American Alpine Institute** — Cascades-based, full alpine programs
- **Single guided Cosmiques Arête day (Chamonix, €400–600)** — refresher on arrival, see [[Guides & Operators]]

## Crevasse rescue drill (the critical one)

Must be able to set up a **3:1 Z-pulley** from arrest in **under 5 minutes**.

1. Self-arrest, dig in hard, build initial anchor (picket or buried axe)
2. Transfer load to anchor with prussik on rope
3. Take in slack rope from victim side
4. Set up 3:1 mechanical advantage:
   - Anchor → climber rope
   - Prussik on victim side rope → carabiner → pulley/biner back to anchor
   - Pull free strand to haul
5. Reset prussik when it reaches the redirect carabiner; haul again
6. Communicate with victim — if they can ascend on their own, much faster

**Drill:** simulate fall with 80 kg of pack/sled hanging off carabiner over a ledge or strong tree branch. Practice until reflexive.

## Steep snow front-pointing technique

- **Crampons:** plant front points firmly, weight directly over them
- **Axe:** dagger position (low slopes), self-belay above shoulder (mid slopes), pied a plat (low-angle ice)
- **Pace:** kick-step rhythm, 3 points of contact ideally
- **Practice:** any steep snow couloir on a Colorado 14er in spring

## Day-of fitness markers

If these are easy, you're ready:
- 4,000 ft gain with 30 lb pack in under 3 hours
- 1,000 m vertical in under 1:00 (no pack)
- Lead 4th-class snow on 45° crampons cleanly
- Sleep through a night at 12,000+ ft without major headache
