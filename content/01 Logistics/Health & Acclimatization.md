# Health & Acclimatization

## Altitude profile across the three trips

| Trip | Summit | Days at altitude | Acclimatization mode |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Grand Teton OS | 13,775 ft / 4,199 m | 2 | Drive-up start (6k ft TH), one night at 11,600 ft. No real acclimatization. |
| Mont Blanc Trois Monts | 15,777 ft / 4,808 m | 2 | One night at 3,613 m + summit push. Borderline — many do warm-up climbs (Aig du Tour) first. |
| Denali West Buttress | 20,310 ft / 6,191 m | 14–21 | Full classic profile — climb high, sleep low, multi-week. |

Living at altitude in **Denver (1,609 m)** is meaningful pre-acclimatization. Not enough to skip steps, but a real head start vs sea-level climbers.

## Pre-trip altitude prep (cross-cutting)

- **Live high, train low** isn't realistic for most, but **sleeping in altitude tents** (Hypoxico) for 4–6 weeks before Denali is a documented advantage for ~$300–500/month rental
- **Cardio base:** the strongest acclimatizer is excellent aerobic fitness. Your 50K ultra training is already doing this.
- **Hike high days:** Front Range 14ers (Quandary, Bierstadt, Grays/Torreys) — get to 14k+ on day-hikes the month before any altitude trip
- **Avoid:** new diet, new supplements, alcohol the week before
- **Sleep:** prioritize sleep aggressively in the 2 weeks before. Best resilience marker.

## Medications

### Acetazolamide (Diamox)
- **Standard prophylactic** for AMS prevention
- **Dose:** 125 mg twice daily starting 24 h before ascent above 2,500 m, continued until at high point or descent begins
- **Side effects:** finger tingling, mild diuresis, carbonated drinks taste flat. Sulfa allergy = avoid.
- **Recommendation:** carry for all three, use selectively. **Denali: yes, prophylactic.** **Mont Blanc: optional.** **Teton: no, you can taste-test it on a Front Range 14er to know your reaction.**

### Dexamethasone (Decadron)
- **Emergency treatment** for HACE and severe AMS
- 4 mg every 6 h, doctor-prescribed only
- Carry for **Denali**; talk to a travel-medicine physician

### Nifedipine
- **Emergency treatment** for HAPE
- 30 mg extended-release every 12 h
- Carry for **Denali**, prescribed

### General travel meds (all trips)
- Ibuprofen / acetaminophen (rotate, don't stack)
- Imodium
- Cipro or azithromycin (traveler's diarrhea, if relevant — less needed for these trips than Asia/SA)
- Personal Rx + extras + photos of labels
- Eye drops (dry alpine air)
- Lip balm with SPF
- Sunscreen — **mineral, high SPF**; reapply every 2 h on glacier

## Sun protection — the underrated killer

On glacier the **reflected UV** can permanently blind unprepared eyes (snow blindness, photokeratitis). On Denali this risk is extreme — high latitude, long days, snow everywhere, reflective at all angles.

- **Glacier glasses, Cat 4 lenses, side shields** — non-negotiable for Mont Blanc and Denali. Cheap polarized sunglasses are NOT enough.
- **Backup pair** — losing your primary on Denali is a trip-ender. Carry a spare.
- **Goggles** — required for Denali storms; nice-to-have for Mont Blanc

## Acclimatization strategy per trip
See [[Acclimatization Strategy]] for the full plan. Summary:

- **Teton:** drive over Snowy Range or stop at a Front Range 14er the week before. Sleep in Jackson (1,925 m), hike in, sleep Lower Saddle (3,540 m), summit and descend next day. Marginal acclimatization but the climb is short.
- **Mont Blanc:** if doable, arrive Chamonix 4–5 days early, do Aiguille du Tour (3,540 m) or Petit Aiguille Verte → sleep Cosmiques (3,613 m) → summit. Many do back-to-back acclimatization climbs out of Cosmiques first.
- **Denali:** the route IS the acclimatization. 17–21 days, never gain more than ~3,000 ft of sleeping elevation per day after Camp 3, rest days at 14k Camp, only push to high camp when ready.

## Hydration

- 4–5 L/day at altitude. Snowmelt water at higher camps — bring chlorine dioxide or a backup filter.
- **Denali fuel math:** every L of water above ~14,000 ft requires ~10 min of stove time + fuel. This is why fuel planning matters — see [[Denali Gear List]].

## Frostbite / cold injury

- **Hand-warmer chemistry doesn't work below ~–25°F.** At Denali's 17k Camp and summit day, chemical warmers are placebo.
- **Vapor-barrier liners** in boots and gloves at extreme cold — keeps insulation dry over multi-day pushes
- **The buddy check:** climbing partner watches your nose, cheeks, ears. White spots = stop, rewarm. Don't be brave about this.
- **Boot fit:** the most common cause of Denali toe loss is boots that were "fine in the store" but became too tight with thick socks at altitude. **Pair boots with the exact socks you'll climb in, walk in them for hours.**

## Nutrition

- **Calories:** ~3,000–3,500/day on Mont Blanc, **4,500–5,500/day** on Denali (above 14k, appetite drops — eat anyway)
- **Pre-planned food bags per camp day:** prevents standing in spindrift digging for snacks
- **Hot drink rotation:** tea, cocoa, broth, instant coffee — boredom is the appetite-killer, variety helps
- **Real fat sources:** olive oil shots, butter, salami — dense calories that travel well

## Mental health on long trips

- Denali is psychologically hard. 21 days, weather days in a tent, tension with partners.
- **Strategies:** book/audiobook on phone, weight-budgeted; pre-agreed quiet hours; rotate cook duties to break routine
- **Talking through plan changes:** decide before the trip how you'll handle "one person wants to bail" scenarios. Pre-mortem the social dynamics.
