# Accommodations

## Grand Teton — Owen-Spalding

### Pre/post climb (Jackson area)
- **Climber's Ranch** (American Alpine Club) — bunkhouse on-park, $40–50/night for AAC members. Climbing crowd, kitchen, showers. Reserve early summer for July–Aug. [AAC Climbers Ranch](https://americanalpineclub.org/climbers-ranch)
- **Gros Ventre Campground** — NPS, $35/night, first-come-first-served (mostly), only ~15 min to Lupine Meadows. Hot in Aug.
- **Jackson hotels** — pricey ($250+ summer). Cheaper options in **Driggs, ID** over Teton Pass (~45 min) or **Victor, ID**.
- **Dispersed camping:** Bridger-Teton NF outside park — free, no facilities. Shadow Mountain area is popular.

### On the climb
- **Lower Saddle (11,600 ft)** — standard high camp. National Park backcountry permit required. Exum has a permanent yurt — only Exum clients (or guides) get inside.
- **The Meadows (9,200 ft)** — lower bivy option, more often used for descent / weather days.
- **Permits:** must be reserved in advance via [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/) — see [[Permits & Registration]].

## Mont Blanc — Trois Monts

### Chamonix valley (pre/post climb)
- **Refuge town:** Chamonix proper has everything; Argentière (5 km north) and Les Houches (5 km south) are quieter, cheaper, on the same train line
- **Recommended budget:** [Gîte Le Vagabond](https://gitevagabond.com/) (dorm, climber crowd), [Auberge de Jeunesse Chamonix](https://www.hifrance.org/), [Camping Les Marmottes](https://www.lesmarmottes-camping.com/) (open summer only)
- **Mid-range:** [Hôtel Le Chamonix](https://www.hotelchamonix.com/), [Hôtel Faucigny](https://hotelfaucigny-chamonix.com/) — ~€100–180/night summer
- **Cooking up:** most chalets/gîtes have shared kitchens. Supermarche Casino in Chamonix center; bigger Carrefour up valley

### On the climb — Refuge du Cosmiques (3,613 m)
- **The standard launch for Trois Monts.** Reached from Aiguille du Midi téléphérique upper station via the **arête du Midi** (~30 min exposed snow ridge — short rope, crampons on)
- **Booking is mandatory and competitive.** Opens ~6 months ahead. Half-board ~€90/night. Allocated dinner times; sleeping is dorm-style.
- [Refuge des Cosmiques booking](https://www.refugecosmiques.com/)
- **Alternative:** Refuge du Goûter (3,835 m) is for the **Goûter route**, not Trois Monts. Different climb.
- **Bivy:** not really an option for Trois Monts — exposed glacier terrain, refuge is the only sensible option

### Téléphérique
- **Aiguille du Midi cable car** runs Chamonix (1,035 m) → Plan de l'Aiguille (2,317 m) → Aiguille du Midi (3,777 m). ~20 min total.
- **Cost:** ~€75 round-trip. Book online to skip queues.
- **First lift in summer ~06:30** — too late for an early start; plan to **sleep at Cosmiques and start ~01:30**
- [Compagnie du Mont Blanc](https://www.montblancnaturalresort.com/)

## Denali — West Buttress

### Anchorage (transit nights)
- **Pre-climb:** stay near the airport or downtown for the gear/food shop. Budget: Black Spruce, Earth B&B, Sourdough Lodge. Mid-range: Hilton, Hyatt.
- **AMH (Alaska Mountaineering & Hiking)** — gear shop in Anchorage. Stop here for last-minute fuel, stove parts, fresh batteries. [AMH website](https://www.alaskamountaineering.com/)

### Talkeetna (1–3 nights minimum)
- **Required:** in-person check-in at the **Walter Harper Talkeetna Ranger Station** before flying in
- **Talkeetna Roadhouse** — climbing institution. Bunkhouse rooms ~$30–50, private rooms $100+, breakfast is famous. Book ahead, very popular. [Talkeetna Roadhouse](https://talkeetnaroadhouse.com/)
- **Latitude 62 Lodge** — alternative, cheaper, bare-bones
- **Air taxi cabins:** TAT and K2 both have lodge / bunkhouse options for clients while waiting on weather

### On the mountain — 8 camps over 17–21 days
| Camp | Elevation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base camp (SE Fork Kahiltna) | 7,200 ft / 2,200 m | Fly-in point. Camp here only the night you arrive. |
| Ski Hill camp | 7,800 ft / 2,380 m | Most teams skip — push directly to Camp 1 |
| **Camp 1 (Kahiltna Pass)** | 7,800 ft / 2,380 m | Cache and move day, 5 mi from base |
| **Camp 2** | 9,500 ft / 2,900 m | "11 Camp" approach, common cache stop |
| **Camp 3 — 11,200 Camp** | 11,200 ft / 3,400 m | First major camp. Most spend 1 acclimatization day. |
| **Camp 4 — 14,200 Camp (Genet Basin)** | 14,200 ft / 4,330 m | The acclimatization base. **Plan 4–7 days here.** Cache on fixed lines, rest day, push to high camp when weather + body align. |
| **Camp 5 — 17,200 / High Camp** | 17,200 ft / 5,240 m | Cold, windy. Summit from here. Don't sit more than 2 nights — burning oxygen, getting weaker. |
| Summit | 20,310 ft / 6,190 m | |

- **Camp building:** every camp involves digging in, building snow walls. Plan 1–2h of camp construction per move.
- **Caching:** standard pattern is "carry high, sleep low" — move loads up, return, sleep, then move camp up. This is the acclimatization rhythm.
