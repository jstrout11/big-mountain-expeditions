# Weather & Conditions — Denali

## Climbing season

| Month | Climbable | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| April (late) | Yes | Cold (–30 to –50 °F nights), short days, fewer crowds, stable snowpack |
| **May** | **Yes — peak** | Lengthening days, cold but warming, best snowpack typically |
| **June** | **Yes — peak** | Long days (20 h light), warmer, busiest, often best summit success rates |
| **July (early)** | **Yes** | Crevasses opening up, route becoming harder, fewer climbers |
| July (late) | Marginal | Heavily crevassed Kahiltna, route quality poor |
| Aug–Apr | No | Winter / spring conditions |

`denali_target_window:: mid-May to mid-June`

## Summit success rates (NPS data)

- ~50% of all teams summit
- ~60% of experienced unguided teams summit
- ~75% of guided teams summit (more weather flexibility, can pin down longer)
- Teams that allocate **24+ days** for the trip summit more often than teams that allocate 18

The single biggest driver: **weather window at High Camp**. Teams that get a 36-hour clear weather window above 17 Camp typically summit. Teams that don't, don't.

## Primary forecast sources

- **[NWS Anchorage Denali Forecast](https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php?lat=63.0692&lon=-151.0070)**: official US service for Denali coordinates. **Use the 4-day forecast + zone discussion.**
- **[Alaska Mountain Forecast (NWS)](https://www.weather.gov/aprfc/AlaskaMountainForecast)**: aggregator for Alaska's high peaks
- **[Mountain Forecast Denali](https://www.mountain-forecast.com/peaks/Mount-Denali/forecasts/6190)**: elevation-resolved, 7-day, free
- **[NWS Anchorage Forecast Discussion](https://forecast.weather.gov/product.php?site=NWS&issuedby=AFC&product=AFD)**: technical forecaster narrative — sets expectations for the next 4–5 days
- **NPS Denali Mountaineering Bulletins**: posted at Base Camp and at 14 Camp ranger station, daily during climbing season — **this is the source rangers use**
- **[Weatherspark Denali climate](https://weatherspark.com/y/410/Average-Weather-at-Mount-Denali-Alaska-United-States-Year-Round)**: climatology, useful for understanding norms

## What forecasters look for

### Pressure patterns
- **Stable ridge over Alaska Range** = high pressure, light winds, clear → climb
- **Aleutian low approaching** = storm system in 24–48 h → wait
- **Pacific moisture lift over the range** = storm + heavy snow → wait

### Specific parameters at altitude
- **500 mb height** over Talkeetna: 552 dm+ = ridge, climb; 540 dm or below = trough, storm
- **Wind at 17,000 ft (520 mb level)**: <20 kt = climb; 20–40 kt = uncomfortable, marginal; 40+ kt = no-go
- **Temperature at summit**: –30 °C summer typical; –40 °C = serious cold, plan accordingly

## Weather phenomena specific to Denali

### Kahiltna Glacier "thermals"
- Sun-driven afternoon winds blowing down the Kahiltna — annoying but not dangerous on the lower glacier
- Best to break camp early and travel mornings on the lower mountain

### High-altitude wind events
- **Jet stream interaction** with Alaska Range can produce summit-level winds of 80+ mph for days
- Teams stuck at High Camp through these events are common; this is when buffer days save the trip
- **Lenticular clouds** over the summit = high winds aloft, almost always storm coming

### Cold snap events
- 2–3 day deep cold pushes (–50 °F at high camp, summit at –60 °F) happen 1–2× per season
- Frostbite injuries spike during these — pinned-down teams get cold while waiting
- Tent-time strategy: dry clothes, hydrate, eat, sleep, don't open the tent unnecessarily

### Whiteouts on the upper mountain
- Persistent fog/whiteout above 14 Camp common during storm cycles
- **Wand the route** between camps — you can't navigate visually in fog
- 14 Camp → 17 Camp move = waypoint everything

## Practical decision framework

| Forecast feature | Action |
|---|---|
| 4+ day clear window, summit wind <25 mph, temps "warm" (–25 °C) | **Summit push** — move 14→17, summit next day |
| 2-day window, summit wind 25–35 mph | Marginal — climb only if at High Camp; from 14 Camp, wait |
| Storm in 24 h, currently stable | Move to next higher camp if it's still stable; don't go for summit |
| Active storm, 36+ h system | Pin at 14 Camp or 17 Camp; hunker; conserve fuel |
| 5+ days of bad weather forecast | Consider descent to lower camp; bad weather above 17 Camp burns climbers out |

## On-mountain weather sources

- **Ranger camp at 14,200 ft** — daily NPS-posted weather updates (printed weather chart, text discussion)
- **Satellite phone** — air taxi operators relay forecasts to camp. Many parties carry their own sat phone.
- **inReach with weather subscription** — bring this. Premium subscription includes 7-day point forecasts.
- **NOAA Forecast via Iridium** — some teams subscribe to a custom Alaska Range product

## Pre-trip weather strategy

- **2 weeks out:** start tracking the synoptic pattern; long-term ridges/troughs
- **3 days out (last sleep in Anchorage):** detailed forecast through fly-in day
- **Fly-in day:** decision lies with air taxi operator, not you — they fly when the Kahiltna landing strip is flyable

## When to skip the season

- **Fly-out delays of 5+ days:** if Talkeetna is socked in, you may sit on the glacier or in Talkeetna for nearly a week. Some years more.
- **Spring with abnormal warmth:** crevasses open earlier, sled hauling becomes harder, summit success drops. 2024 was a notable example.
- **Late-season heat wave:** rare but documented; affects the upper mountain via melting/soft snow conditions
