# Training & Skills — Denali

## Demand profile

- **Duration:** 21 days on glacier, hauling 60–80 lb sled + 60–70 lb pack
- **Altitude:** 6,191 m / 20,310 ft summit, physiologically ~7,000 m due to latitude
- **Cold:** –40 °F potential, –20 °F routine at high camp
- **Technical:** glacier travel + crevasse rescue + fixed-line ascending + steep snow + camp construction

`denali_training_lead_time_months:: 6-12`

## Lead time

**12 months ideal**, 6 months minimum after a solid base. Carryover from 50K ultra training is strong on aerobic base; **needs added load-carry strength specifically**.

## 12-month calendar

| Month | Phase | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| -12 | Off-season | Maintain base, recover, ski |
| -10 | Base | Build aerobic mileage, gym strength 3x/wk |
| -8 | Base | Add long hikes 4–5 hr, climb a Front Range 14er |
| -6 | Base→Build | Start loaded pack hikes, increase strength |

## Specific training

### Pack carries (critical)
- Weekly long hikes with 50–70 lb pack, 6–10 hours, 3,000–5,000 ft gain
- Build to **70 lb / 8 hours / 4,000 ft** comfortably by 3 months out
- **Back-to-back** Sat + Sun pack days monthly in build phase

### Sled drag simulation
- Tire drag on dirt roads (truck tire + harness)
- Loaded jogger stroller on packed snow
- Weighted backpack as proxy (less faithful)
- Hard to find, but valuable

### Altitude exposure
- Repeated 14er exposure all summer/fall before trip
- Consider altitude tent (Hypoxico) 4–6 weeks pre-trip for sleep adaptation
- **Cotopaxi / Chimborazo** count strongly; **Aconcagua** is gold standard if doable
- Pre-Denali "shakedown" trip to Rainier or a 17–19k S. American peak in the prior year

### Cold exposure
- Practice sleeping out at **–20 °F** at least 3 nights
- Test full layering system in actual cold
- Test stove + cooking with mittens at temps below freezing

### Skills (drilled within 90 days of trip — see also below)

## Strength essentials

- **Back squat** — work to 1.5× body weight for 5 reps
- **Deadlift** — work to 1.75× body weight for 5 reps
- **Bulgarian split squat** — 30 lb in each hand for 8 reps each leg
- **Step-ups** — 24" box, 30 lb each hand, 8 reps each leg
- **Pull-ups** — 10 strict for body weight
- **Single-leg deadlift** — 50% of regular deadlift for 8 reps each leg
- **Loaded carry** (farmer's, sled drag) — 30+ min weekly

## Cardio benchmarks

- **Vertical kilometer test:** climb 1,000 m of elevation gain, fastest you can. Sub-1:00 strong, sub-1:15 reasonable.
- **Loaded vertical:** 1,000 m gain with 50 lb pack — target sub-2:00
- **Heart-rate-controlled climb:** climb 3,000 ft with HR capped at 75% max — should feel sustainable for 6+ hours

## Technical skill checklist (drilled within 90 days of trip)

- [ ] **Crevasse rescue 3:1:** set up from arrest in <5 min, drilled with rope partner
- [ ] **Self-arrest:** drilled in 4 positions on real snow within 30 days
- [ ] **Steep snow front-pointing:** comfortable on 45–55° snow with crampons + axe
- [ ] **Fixed-line ascending:** smooth transitions at intermediate anchors with Petzl Ascension
- [ ] **Snow anchor:** picket (vertical + horizontal/deadman), T-slot, buried axe
- [ ] **Camp building:** snow walls, tent platform, anchor system — drill in winter
- [ ] **Stove + cooking with mittens** at sub-freezing temps
- [ ] **Knot review:** figure-8 follow-through, butterfly, prussik, Bachmann, clove, Munter

## Crevasse rescue drill — the critical one

Must be able to set up a **3:1 Z-pulley** from arrest in **under 5 minutes**.

1. Self-arrest, dig in hard, build initial anchor (picket or buried axe)
2. Transfer load to anchor with prussik on rope
3. Take in slack rope from victim side
4. Set up 3:1 mechanical advantage:
   - Anchor → climber rope
   - Prussik on victim side rope → carabiner → pulley/biner back to anchor
   - Pull free strand to haul
5. Reset prussik when it reaches the redirect carabiner; haul again
6. Communicate with victim — if they can ascend on their own, much faster

**Drill:** simulate fall with 80 kg of pack/sled hanging off carabiner over a ledge or strong tree branch. Practice until reflexive.

## Fixed-line ascending (Denali specific)

- **Petzl Ascension** smooth on/off transitions
- **Body position** on steep ground: feet planted, ascender below shoulder
- **Rappel transitions** at intermediate anchors
- Find a steep practice fixed line on a cliff or via ferrata-style setup
- The **Headwall fixed lines** (14k → 16k) are 1,400 ft of fixed rope — practice running ascender repeatedly without arm fatigue

## Camp building (Denali-specific)

- **Snow walls:** quarry blocks 50 × 30 × 20 cm with snow saw, stack to wind-block
- **Tent platform:** stomp out flat area 3 × 3 m, let cure 30 min before pitching
- **Anchor system:** every guyline + main stake = picket, T-slot, or carabiner-clipped axe
- **Vestibule for cooking + storage**

## Where to drill

### Front Range (Colorado) practice locations
- **St Mary's Glacier (45 min from Denver)** — small but real glacier surface for drills
- **Holy Cross, Quandary, Castle 14ers in spring** — steep snow couloirs
- **Local climbing gym** — anchor building, multi-pitch transitions, rappel practice
- **Backyard / garage** — fixed-rope ascension setup, prussik knot practice
- **Brainard Lake winter** — snow camping, cold sleep test

### Pre-trip course options
- **Colorado Mountain School** — multi-day glacier travel + crevasse rescue
- **American Alpine Institute** — Cascades-based, full alpine programs; AAI runs **Denali Prep courses** specifically
- **Mountain Trip Denali Prep** — winter weeks in Alaska + Cascades

## Day-of fitness markers

If these are easy, you're ready:
- 70 lb pack + 4,000 ft gain in 6 hours without trashing yourself
- 1,000 m vertical (no pack) in under 1 hour
- 50 lb sled drag + 30 lb pack for 4 hours on snow
- Sleep through a –20 °F night in your test setup
