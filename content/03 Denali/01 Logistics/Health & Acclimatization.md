## The latitude effect

Denali at **62° N** has atmospheric pressure at altitude **lower than equatorial peaks**. Physiologically, Denali's summit feels like a **23,000–24,000 ft (7,000–7,300 m)** Himalayan peak. Treat the altitude with corresponding respect.

## Acclimatization strategy — the route IS the acclimatization

The 17–21 day West Buttress profile is built around classic acclimatization principles:

### Sleeping altitude progression (target)

| Day | Camp | Elevation | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Base | 2,200 m | Fly in afternoon |
| 2 | Camp 1 | 2,400 m | First haul day |
| 3 | Camp 2 | 2,900 m | Move + cache |
| 4 | Camp 2 | 2,900 m | Rest / weather |
| 5–6 | Camp 3 (11k) | 3,400 m | Cache up to 13k, move up |
| 7 | Camp 3 | 3,400 m | Rest |
| 8 | Camp 4 (14k) | 4,330 m | Move day |
| **9–11** | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | **Acclimatization base — 3+ nights** |
| 12 | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | Cache to 16k via fixed lines, return |
| 13 | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | Rest |
| 14 | High Camp | 5,240 m | Move day via West Buttress ridge |
| 15 | High Camp | 5,240 m | Summit day OR rest |
| 16+ | Descend | | Reverse direction |

### Key mitigation tactics

- **Carry high, sleep low:** the fundamental rhythm. Cache loads up, return to sleep at lower camp, then move camp up the next day.
- **Diamox prophylactic** above Camp 2 — common practice (125 mg twice daily)
- **Three nights minimum at 14 Camp** before going to High Camp — non-negotiable
- **Maximum 2 nights at High Camp** — strength bleeds away above 17,000 ft
- **Listen to the rangers** at 14 Camp medical station — they see everyone, know when to call off

## Pre-trip altitude prep

- **Live high, train low** isn't realistic for most, but **sleeping in altitude tents** (Hypoxico) for 4–6 weeks before is documented advantage for ~$300–500/month rental
- **Cardio base:** the strongest acclimatizer is excellent aerobic fitness. Your 50K ultra training is doing this.
- **Hike high days:** Front Range 14ers (Quandary, Bierstadt, Grays/Torreys) — get to 14k+ on day-hikes the month before
- **Aconcagua or Chimborazo within prior year** is ideal altitude experience
- **Avoid:** new diet, new supplements, alcohol the week before

## Medications to carry

### Standard
- **Acetazolamide (Diamox) 125 mg** × 30 tabs — prophylactic + treatment
- **Ibuprofen** × 40, **Tylenol** × 20, **Aspirin** × 12

### Doctor-prescribed (essential for Denali)
- **Dexamethasone (Decadron) 4 mg** × 10 tabs — HACE emergency
- **Nifedipine 30 mg ER** × 10 — HAPE emergency
- **Cipro** or azithromycin × 12 — backup antibiotic

### Other
- **Imodium** × 8
- **Diphenhydramine** (sleep, allergy)
- **Personal Rx + 30% extras + photos of labels**

## Sun protection — the underrated killer at high latitude

On Denali, **reflected UV from snow at high latitude long days** is extreme. **Snow blindness** is a real trip-ender.

- **Glacier glasses, Cat 4 lenses, side shields** — non-negotiable. Julbo Vermont or Spektrum Östra.
- **Backup pair** — losing your primary at 17 Camp is a trip-ender. Carry a spare.
- **Goggles** — mandatory for storms; Cat 3 or 4
- **Sunscreen mineral SPF 50** — 3 × 100 ml tubes for trip
- **Lip balm SPF** — chronic chap; ×3

## Frostbite / cold injury

> [!warning] Chemistry doesn't work below –25 °F
> Hand-warmer chemical packets don't generate heat below ~–25 °F. At Denali's 17k Camp and summit day, chemical warmers are placebo.

- **Vapor-barrier liners** in boots and gloves at extreme cold — keeps insulation dry over multi-day pushes
- **The buddy check:** climbing partner watches your nose, cheeks, ears. White spots = stop, rewarm. **Don't be brave about this.**
- **Boot fit:** the most common cause of Denali toe loss is boots that were "fine in the store" but became too tight with thick socks at altitude. **Pair boots with the exact socks you'll climb in, walk in them for hours.**

## Hydration

- **4–5 L/day at altitude**
- **Snowmelt water** at higher camps — bring chlorine dioxide or backup filter
- **Denali fuel math:** every L of water above ~14,000 ft requires ~10 min of stove time + fuel. This is why fuel planning matters — see [[Gear List]].

## Nutrition

- **Calories:** ~4,500–5,500/day on Denali (above 14k, appetite drops — eat anyway)
- **Pre-planned food bags per camp day:** prevents standing in spindrift digging for snacks
- **Hot drink rotation:** tea, cocoa, broth, instant coffee — variety helps
- **Real fat sources:** olive oil shots, butter, salami — dense calories that travel well

## Mental health on long trips

- Denali is psychologically hard. 21 days, weather days in a tent, tension with partners.
- **Strategies:** book/audiobook on phone, weight-budgeted; pre-agreed quiet hours; rotate cook duties to break routine
- **Talking through plan changes:** decide before the trip how you'll handle "one person wants to bail" scenarios. Pre-mortem the social dynamics.

## Warning signs to descend

| Symptom | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mild headache, mild fatigue, mild appetite loss | AMS | Stop ascent, hydrate, rest day |
| Persistent moderate headache, nausea, vomiting | Moderate AMS | Diamox, descend if not improving in 12 h |
| **Ataxia (can't walk a straight line)** | **HACE** | **Descend immediately, dex 8 mg, oxygen if available** |
| **Wet cough, frothy/pink sputum, breathlessness at rest** | **HAPE** | **Descend immediately, nifedipine 30 mg ER, oxygen if available** |
| Cyanosis (blue lips/fingertips) | Severe | Descend + medication |
| Altered mental status | Severe | Descend + medication |
