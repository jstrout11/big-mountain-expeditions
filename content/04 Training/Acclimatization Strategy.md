# Acclimatization Strategy

Altitude adaptation is a function of **time + elevation gradient + rest**. The classic rule: above 2,500 m, **don't sleep more than 300–500 m higher** than the previous night, with a **rest day every 1,000 m gained**. Reality is messier; here's how each trip handles it.

## Denver baseline

Living in Denver (~1,609 m / 5,280 ft) confers real but limited pre-acclimatization. Roughly equivalent to **2–3 days of acclimatization** at 2,500 m for someone arriving from sea level. Helpful, not transformative.

## Per-trip strategy

### Grand Teton OS — marginal acclimatization, short trip
- **Profile:** sleep Jackson 1,925 m → trail up to 3,540 m → sleep 3,540 m → summit 4,199 m
- **Issue:** big jump (Lower Saddle to summit in one day from Denver) at modest elevation
- **Mitigation:**
  - Climb one Front Range 14er the week before (Quandary, Bierstadt, Pikes — 4,300+ m)
  - Drive across Snowy Range or Bighorn Pass for a high-pass exposure
  - Hydrate aggressively from arrival in Jackson
  - Optional: 125 mg Diamox bid starting at Jackson, continue through summit day — only if you've tested it on training peaks
- **Expected symptoms:** mild headache common at Lower Saddle. Trouble sleeping. Should not be severe.
- **Risk:** AMS — low. HAPE / HACE — very low.

### Mont Blanc Trois Monts — needs real acclimatization
- **Profile:** Chamonix 1,035 m → Cosmiques 3,613 m → summit 4,808 m. Two nights of altitude.
- **Issue:** big jump from valley to summit, and 4,808 m is genuinely high
- **Mitigation strategies (ranked):**
  1. **Best: 2 acclimatization climbs + Cosmiques + summit (5–6 days)**
     - Day 1: arrive Chamonix, rest
     - Day 2: Aiguille du Tour (3,540 m) — sleep at Refuge Albert Premier (2,706 m)
     - Day 3: descent, rest in Chamonix
     - Day 4: Cosmiques Arête + sleep Cosmiques (3,613 m)
     - Day 5: Trois Monts summit
  2. **Standard: 1 acclimatization day + Cosmiques (4 days)**
     - Day 1: arrive Chamonix
     - Day 2: Plan de l'Aiguille → Petit Aiguille Verte day climb (3,512 m)
     - Day 3: Cosmiques
     - Day 4: summit
  3. **Bare minimum: straight to Cosmiques (3 days)**
     - Day 1: arrive Chamonix
     - Day 2: Cosmiques (sleep at 3,613 m, very compressed)
     - Day 3: summit
- **Pre-trip:** ideally arrive Chamonix from another high mountain trip (e.g., Italian/Swiss Alps) to skip the acclimatization week
- **Expected symptoms:** AMS at Cosmiques is common (40%+ of first-time visitors). HAPE/HACE rare with this profile.

### Denali West Buttress — the route is the acclimatization
- **Profile:** classic 21-day acclimatization curve
- **Sleeping altitude progression** (target — varies by team):

| Day | Camp | Elevation | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Base | 2,200 m | Fly in afternoon |
| 2 | Camp 1 | 2,400 m | First haul day |
| 3 | Camp 2 | 2,900 m | Move + cache |
| 4 | Camp 2 | 2,900 m | Rest / weather |
| 5–6 | Camp 3 (11k) | 3,400 m | Cache up to 13k, move up |
| 7 | Camp 3 | 3,400 m | Rest |
| 8 | Camp 4 (14k) | 4,330 m | Move day |
| 9–11 | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | **Acclimatization base — 3+ nights** |
| 12 | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | Cache to 16k via fixed lines, return |
| 13 | Camp 4 | 4,330 m | Rest |
| 14 | High Camp | 5,240 m | Move day via West Buttress ridge |
| 15 | High Camp | 5,240 m | Summit day OR rest |
| 16+ | Descend | | Reverse direction |

- **Mitigation tactics:**
  - **Carry high, sleep low:** the fundamental rhythm. Cache loads up, return to sleep at lower camp, then move camp up the next day. Adds days but the acclimatization payoff is huge.
  - **Diamox prophylactic** above Camp 2 — common practice
  - **Three nights minimum at 14 Camp** before going to High Camp — non-negotiable
  - **Maximum 2 nights at High Camp** — strength bleeds away above 17,000 ft
  - **Listen to the rangers** at 14 Camp medical station — they see everyone, know when to call off
- **Risk:** AMS — universal. HAPE — meaningful risk above 14 Camp. HACE — rare but lethal. Treatment = descent + dex + nifedipine + oxygen if available.

## Diamox protocol (general)

- **Dose:** 125 mg twice daily (NOT 250 mg twice daily — older guideline, current evidence supports lower dose)
- **Start:** 24 h before crossing 2,500 m
- **Continue:** until at high point or descending
- **Test before trip:** 1–2 doses on a training day — confirm no allergic reaction, learn side effects (tingling, taste change)
- **NOT a replacement for proper acclimatization** — speeds adaptation, doesn't bypass need

## Hypoxic tents (Hypoxico / Altitude Training Systems)

- Worth considering for Denali specifically
- Rent for $300–500/month
- **Protocol:** sleep at simulated 3,500–4,500 m for **4–6 weeks** before trip; 8 hours/night
- Evidence is mixed but generally positive for **time-to-acclimatization on arrival** rather than absolute summit performance
- More relevant if you live at sea level. From Denver, the marginal benefit is smaller.

## Warning signs to descend

| Symptom | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mild headache, mild fatigue, mild appetite loss | AMS | Stop ascent, hydrate, rest day |
| Persistent moderate headache, nausea, vomiting | Moderate AMS | Diamox, descend if not improving in 12 h |
| **Ataxia (can't walk a straight line)** | **HACE** | **Descend immediately, dex 8 mg, oxygen if available** |
| **Wet cough, frothy/pink sputum, breathlessness at rest** | **HAPE** | **Descend immediately, nifedipine 30 mg ER, oxygen if available** |
| Cyanosis (blue lips/fingertips) | Severe | Descend + medication |
| Altered mental status | Severe | Descend + medication |
