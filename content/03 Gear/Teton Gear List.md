# Teton Gear List

Target: **climbing pack 35–40 L, ~7–9 kg** for the summit day from Lower Saddle. Approach pack ~50 L, 12–14 kg with bivy gear.

## Climbing hardware
- Single 70 m, 9.4–9.8 mm dynamic rope
- Light alpine harness (Petzl Sitta)
- Helmet (Petzl Sirocco or Meteor)
- ATC Guide / Reverso 4
- Locking carabiners × 4
- Standard carabiners × 8
- Alpine draws × 6
- 60 cm slings × 4 (sewn dyneema)
- 120 cm sling × 2
- Cordelette (5 mm × 5 m or 6 mm × 6 m)
- **Rack:** set of nuts (BD #5–13), single set cams 0.4–2 (BD Camalots or equivalent), one #3 if you tend to over-protect chimneys
- **Bail material:** 5m of 7mm cord + tape, in case rappel anchor needs replacing
- Belay gloves: optional, helpful in the chimney
- Approach shoes OR climbing shoes for the pitches (see [[Grand Teton — Owen-Spalding#Decisions to make|decision]])

## Snow / ice (conditional — late July+ usually not needed)
- Light steel crampons (BD Sabretooth or similar) — only if early season and snow on route
- 50 cm walking ice axe — same caveat

## Clothing — climb day
- Synthetic base layer top
- Light hiking pants (Patagonia Quandary, Outdoor Research Ferrosi)
- Sun hoodie or long-sleeve T (Patagonia Capilene Cool Daily Hoodie)
- Lightweight fleece or R1 (Patagonia R1 Air Hoodie)
- **Wind / soft shell jacket** — Patagonia Houdini Air, Black Diamond Alpine Start
- Hard shell jacket (kept in pack — only out for storm)
- Light puffy (Patagonia Nano Puff, Mountain Hardwear Ghost Whisperer)
- Beanie + sun hat
- Light gloves (Black Diamond Mont Blanc or similar)
- Climbing socks: smartwool light + sock liner

## Camping (Lower Saddle bivy)
- 15–20 °F down bag (Western Mountaineering UltraLite 20)
- Inflatable pad (Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite NXT)
- 1-person bivy or 2-person ultralight tent shared (Big Agnes Copper Spur HV UL2)
- Lightweight foam sit pad
- Light pack 35–45 L for summit day

## Stove / food
- MSR Pocket Rocket Deluxe or BRS-3000T + canister (1 small per person for 2 days)
- Titanium pot 750 ml
- Spoon
- 2 days of food: breakfasts (instant oats, coffee), 2 dinners (Mountain House), trail snacks (Snickers, jerky, Cliff bars), summit-day food bars × 4

## Water
- 2 × 1L Nalgene OR Smartwater bottles
- Aquatabs as backup (the Lower Saddle spring is potable when flowing — confirm with rangers)
- 3L hydration on approach if hot

## Personal
- Cat 3 sunglasses (no need for full glacier glasses — minor snow exposure)
- Sunscreen + lip balm (small tubes)
- Headlamp + 1 spare battery set
- Phone in waterproof case
- Wallet, cards, $40 cash, license
- AAC card (for ranch + emergency)
- Permit printout (Lower Saddle)
- First aid kit:
  - Ibuprofen × 12, Tylenol × 6
  - Pepto / Imodium
  - Bandaids, leukotape (foot blisters)
  - 3" elastic wrap
  - Athletic tape
  - Tweezers
  - Tiny tube of antibiotic ointment
  - Personal Rx
- Spare contacts / glasses
- Toothbrush + small toothpaste
- 1 pair lightweight camp shoes / socks for the Saddle
- Small dry bag for food (no bear concerns at Lower Saddle but rodents)
- Emergency: small bivy bag (SOL Escape) — 6 oz lifesaver
- Garmin inReach Mini 2 (optional but recommended — Tetons have spotty cell coverage)

## Pack list mode

**Approach pack (50 L) carries everything.** At Lower Saddle, transfer climbing essentials to a 35 L summit pack:
- Rope, rack, harness, helmet, belay device
- Hard shell jacket, light puffy, gloves, beanie
- Headlamp, sunglasses, sunscreen
- 1 L water + Aquatab spares
- 4 bars / snacks for the day
- First aid kit (small)
- Phone, garmin

Leave at the Saddle camp:
- Sleeping bag, pad, bivy/tent
- Stove, fuel, cook kit
- Spare clothes
- Most of the food

## Pre-trip checklist
- [ ] Boots/shoes broken in (1 long hike minimum)
- [ ] Rack inventoried, cams clean
- [ ] Rope inspected end-to-end, no core shots
- [ ] Helmet not expired (UV cracking)
- [ ] Test belay device + locker function with gloves
- [ ] Practice rappel setup at home wall
