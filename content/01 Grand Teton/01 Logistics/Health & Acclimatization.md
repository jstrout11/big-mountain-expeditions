# Health & Acclimatization — Grand Teton

## Altitude profile

| Stop | Elevation |
|---|---:|
| Denver baseline | 1,609 m / 5,280 ft |
| Jackson | 1,925 m / 6,320 ft |
| Lupine Meadows TH | 2,053 m / 6,732 ft |
| Lower Saddle (sleep) | 3,540 m / 11,600 ft |
| Summit | 4,199 m / 13,775 ft |

`teton_summit_m:: 4199`
`max_sleep_elevation_m:: 3540`

## Acclimatization strategy

Marginal — short trip, one night at altitude. **Living in Denver helps.**

- Climb one Front Range 14er the week before (Quandary, Bierstadt, Pikes — 4,300+ m)
- Drive across Snowy Range or Bighorn Pass for high-pass exposure
- Hydrate aggressively from arrival in Jackson
- Optional: 125 mg Diamox bid starting at Jackson, continue through summit day — only if you've tested it on training peaks

**Expected symptoms:** mild headache common at Lower Saddle. Trouble sleeping. Should not be severe.

**Risk:** AMS — low. HAPE / HACE — very low at this elevation.

## Medications to carry

- **Ibuprofen / acetaminophen** (rotate) — for headache, sore muscles
- **Imodium** — just in case
- **Acetazolamide (Diamox) 125 mg** — optional prophylactic, **test on a training 14er first**
- **Sunscreen mineral SPF 50** — high alpine UV
- **Lip balm with SPF**
- **Eye drops** — dry alpine air
- Personal Rx + extras + photos of labels

## Sun protection

- **Cat 3 sunglasses** are sufficient (no significant snow exposure typically by mid-August)
- **Sunscreen reapply every 2 h** above treeline
- **Hat with brim** for the approach

## Hydration

- 3–4 L on the approach day (it's hot in Garnet Canyon)
- 2 L for the summit push
- **Lower Saddle spring** (Black Dike Spring) often potable — confirm flow with rangers + treat with Aquatabs as backup

## Pre-trip athletic considerations

- Train back-to-back long pack days the month before — see [[Training & Skills]]
- Sleep well the week before; alcohol moderation, no new supplements
- Tape any blister-prone spots with Leukotape pre-emptively at trailhead
