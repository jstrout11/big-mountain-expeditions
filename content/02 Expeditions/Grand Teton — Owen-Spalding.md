# Grand Teton — Owen-Spalding

The classic west-side route on the Grand. **Easiest technical line to the summit**, established 1898 by Spalding, Owen, Petersen, and Shive. Mostly a walk/scramble with two short pitches of 5.4 to 5.6 climbing. The natural choice for a first Grand and a great warm-up for bigger objectives.

`route_grade:: II 5.4`
`length_pitches:: 2 short technical + scramble`
`elevation_gain_ft:: 7000`
`approach_miles:: 12 (round trip)`
`car_to_car_hours:: 14-18 (single push) or split 2 days`
`season_window:: mid-July to mid-September`

## Why this route, why now

| Route | Grade | Days | Style | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **Owen-Spalding** | 5.4 | 2 | trad alpine | Standard. Get this first. |
| Upper Exum Ridge | 5.5/5.7 | 2 | more climbing | Better climbing route, "the" Teton route. Logical follow-up. |
| Petzoldt Ridge / Direct Exum | 5.7+ | 2 | technical | After OS + Upper Exum. |
| North Face / North Ridge | 5.8 mixed | 2–3 | committing | Whole different game. |

The Owen-Spalding earns its place as everyone's first Grand: it gets you to the summit at moderate grade, exposes you to alpine-style logistics (long approach, Lower Saddle bivy, alpine start, mixed-medium summit ridge) without demanding hard climbing.

## Route description

### Day 0 — drive in, sleep in Jackson
- Drive DEN → Jackson (~9 h)
- Sleep at [[Accommodations#Pre-post climb Jackson area|AAC Climbers Ranch]] or Gros Ventre campground
- **Stop at Jenny Lake Ranger Station** afternoon — pick up backcountry permit, ask rangers about current route conditions on OS

### Day 1 — Lupine Meadows → Lower Saddle
- **Distance:** ~6.5 mi, **elevation gain:** ~5,000 ft
- Trailhead: **Lupine Meadows TH** (6,732 ft). Arrive 0500–0600 to get parking.
- Trail through Garnet Canyon — well-graded, with a steep "Caves" section near top
- The Meadows (9,200 ft) → Spalding Falls → fixed ropes / boulder field → **Lower Saddle (11,600 ft)**
- Time: 5–7 hours with overnight pack
- Camp at Lower Saddle. **Bivy spots are limited** — arrive midday, claim one. Stunning sunset on the Middle Teton.
- Hydrate aggressively. The Lower Saddle has a small **spring** (the Black Dike Spring) — verify with rangers it's flowing.

### Day 2 — Lower Saddle → Summit → Lupine Meadows
- **Alpine start: 0300–0400.** Cold, dark, headlamps. Move quietly past Exum camp.
- **Lower Saddle → Upper Saddle** via the *Eye of the Needle* corridor. Class 3 scramble, exposure feels real but trail is obvious in daylight.
- **At Upper Saddle (13,160 ft):** rope up. This is the start of technical climbing.
- **The Belly Roll:** traverse exposed ledge with a notable belly-roll move around a corner. Protect with cams (0.5–2).
- **The Crawl:** horizontal hand-traverse, low on a ledge. Spectacular exposure.
- **The Owen Chimney:** ~50 ft of 5.4 chimney climbing. Stem and friction; not committing but a real pitch.
- **The Sargent Chimney / final 5.6:** short technical move, then 4th-class scramble to summit
- **Summit (13,775 ft):** ~8–10 a.m. if started 0400, weather window typically still good
- **Descent: the rappel.** Standard descent is a **single 120-foot rappel** off the Upper Saddle, anchored to the *double-rope rappel station* below the summit. **Single 70m rope works if you tie off; double-rope rappel preferred** if you brought two.
- Back to Lower Saddle by ~13:00, pack up, descend to TH by ~18:00
- **Single-push option:** strong parties skip the Lower Saddle bivy and do car-to-car in 16–20h. Doable but tough on a Grand with limited acclimatization — recommend split for first time.

## Key gear (highlights — full list in [[Teton Gear List]])

- **Rope:** single 70m, 9.4–9.8 mm dynamic
- **Rack:** light alpine — set of nuts, single cams 0.4–2, 4–6 alpine draws, 2 cordelettes, anchor material
- **Harness:** light alpine harness (Petzl Sitta, BD Couloir, similar)
- **Helmet:** rockfall exposure throughout
- **Approach shoes** for trail; **light alpine boots OR climbing shoes** for the pitches — most parties keep approach shoes on the entire route
- **Crampons + axe:** check current conditions — if there's a snow patch above the Lower Saddle (sometimes lingers into early July), need light steel crampons + axe. **By August, usually dry.**

## Owen-Spalding-specific risks

> [!warning] Afternoon thunderstorms
> Tetons get classic afternoon thunderstorms June–September. **Be off the summit by noon** — no exceptions. Lightning on Upper Saddle is fatal regularly.

> [!warning] Rappel anchor management
> The single-rope rappel point has weathered slings. **Inspect and back up.** Most parties leave a fresh sling. Bring cord/webbing.

> [!warning] Crowding in summer
> July–August weekends, the OS sees 30+ parties on a good-weather day. Plan to **start an hour earlier than the herd** to avoid logjams at the Belly Roll and rappel.

> [!warning] Loose rock on Upper Saddle
> Stuff *will* be kicked down. Helmets stay on. Be careful below parties; consider letting a fast group through.

## Decisions to make

- [ ] **Single day or two-day?** Two-day recommended for first Grand. Note: Single-day skips the permit hassle but extends the car-to-car significantly.
- [ ] **Climbing shoes or approach shoes for the pitches?** Approach shoes faster, climbing shoes more secure on the chimney. 5.4 wet in chimney = climbing shoes worth it.
- [ ] **Solo, partner, or rope team of 3?** Solo on the technical pitches = not recommended unless soloing is your normal thing. Rope team of 3 is slow but lets you take a partner who can't lead. Pair of 2 is ideal.
- [ ] **August or September attempt?** August = warmer, more crowds, more thunderstorm days. September = colder mornings, smaller crowds, more stable weather. **Late-August / early-September is the sweet spot.**

## Pre-trip checklist

- [ ] Backcountry permit reserved via [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/4675317) for Lower Saddle ([[Permits & Registration#Grand Teton]])
- [ ] AAC Climbers Ranch booked (3 nights: drive-in, climb, drive-out)
- [ ] Weather window confirmed — see [[Teton Weather & Conditions]]
- [ ] Rope partner has Grade II 5.4 trad experience or you're prepared to short-rope
- [ ] Rack borrowed/owned, anchor material refreshed
- [ ] Last training: hike Quandary or Bierstadt with pack the weekend before
- [ ] Stop at Jenny Lake Ranger Station for current conditions on day of arrival

## Local guides (if reconsidering)
- **Exum Mountain Guides** — *the* Teton outfit. Hut at Lower Saddle. Full guided OS in 2 days for $1,200–1,500/person (typically 2:1). [exumguides.com](https://www.exumguides.com/)
- **Jackson Hole Mountain Guides** — second major outfit, equally reputable. [jhmg.com](https://www.jhmg.com/)

> [!tip] Skills day with Exum
> Pre-Mont Blanc, consider booking a **single Exum guided day** for crevasse rescue / glacier travel / short-roping skills refresh — about $600/day for 2:1 instruction. Much cheaper than guided Mont Blanc, and great prep.
