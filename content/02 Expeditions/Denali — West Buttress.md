# Denali — West Buttress

The standard route on North America's highest peak — the "easiest" line up Denali, which is to say: still a 17–21 day full expedition with extreme cold, glacier hauling, and altitude problems at sea-level latitudes that hit harder than the elevation number suggests.

**This is the capstone trip.** Don't do this before Mont Blanc.

## The Denali altitude effect

Denali sits at **62° N latitude**. Atmospheric pressure at altitude is lower at high latitudes than at the equator — meaning Denali's **physiological altitude is roughly equivalent to a 23,000–24,000 ft (7,000–7,300 m) peak in the Himalaya**. This is the #1 thing first-timers underestimate.

Combined with **extreme cold** (–40 °F summit days are routine) and **glacier hauling logistics**, Denali is the technical-floor entry point to "real" big-mountain expedition climbing.

## Route — camp by camp

### Stage 0 — fly in
- **Talkeetna Air Taxi / K2 / Sheldon → SE Fork of the Kahiltna Glacier (7,200 ft)**
- Otter or Beaver ski-equipped. ~45 min flight, dramatic landing
- **Base Camp** is a small staging area with seasonal NPS presence, satellite phone, basic toilet area
- Arrive afternoon, build camp, **rest**. Don't move on arrival day.

### Day 1–2 — Base Camp → Camp 1 (Kahiltna Pass, 7,800 ft)
- Distance: ~5 mi, mostly flat
- **First haul day.** You'll be pulling sleds + carrying packs (total ~125 lb between sled and pack per person)
- Drop down "Heartbreak Hill" — a steep but short descent at the start
- Standard rope team of 3 with sleds
- Camp 1 is a typical Kahiltna stop; some teams cache here, sleep at Ski Hill

### Day 3–4 — Camp 1 → Camp 2 (11,200 ft, "11 Camp")
- **Distance:** ~5 mi, gain ~3,400 ft
- Move through the **Kahiltna Glacier** with crevasse exposure throughout
- Steep section up "Motorcycle Hill" before 11 Camp
- **Cache day:** carry partial load to ~10,500 ft and return
- **Move day next:** full pack up to 11 Camp
- Camp 2 is in a flat basin, can hold ~50 climbers in busy May/June

### Day 5–7 — 11 Camp → Camp 3 (14,200 Camp, Genet Basin)
- **The acclimatization push.** Most teams cache once, then move.
- **Cache day:** carry to **Windy Corner (~13,500 ft)** — exposed, often gusty traverse, route varies year to year based on snow
- **Move day:** load up, cross Windy Corner, descend slightly into Genet Basin
- **14,200 Camp** is the home base of the upper mountain. Ranger camp here, weather forecasts posted, medical aid station. Rest days, gear sort, weather watch.

### Day 8–12 — 14 Camp → 17,200 High Camp (via Headwall)
- **Plan 4–7 days at 14 Camp.** Patience here = summit success.
- **Cache day to Windy Corner High** (~16,200 ft cache below fixed lines): full carry up the **headwall fixed lines** (1,400 ft of fixed rope, 40–50° snow with ice patches). Cache at the **top of the fixed lines** (~16,200 ft).
- **Rest day** at 14k.
- **Move day to 17,200 High Camp:** carry full kit up fixed lines, retrieve cache, continue up the West Buttress ridge proper. The buttress ridge from 16,200 → 17,200 is the most exposed climbing on the route — narrow ridge with both-sides drop-offs, easy mixed scrambling. Total move: 6–9 hours.
- **High Camp (17,200 ft)** is cold, windy, often miserable. Don't sit more than 2 nights — strength bleeds away.

### Day 13–15 — Summit push
- **Summit day:** 17,200 → 20,310 → 17,200, 10–14 hours
- Start ~05:00 (mid-summer Alaska daylight = 20+ hours, but the cold of pre-dawn matters)
- **Autobahn:** snow traverse at 18,500 ft. Mostly straightforward but a known fall zone (steep slope below). Some teams place pickets.
- **Denali Pass (18,200 ft):** col between Buttress and summit ridge
- **Football Field (19,500 ft):** flat plateau, deceptive — don't relax, summit ridge still ahead
- **Pig Hill / final headwall:** 600 ft, 30° → flatter
- **Summit ridge:** 200 m walk, often crusty snow, summit
- **Summit (20,310 ft):** brief, photo, descend immediately. Frostbite risk skyrockets with summit lingering.
- **Descent:** the riskiest part of the trip. Most accidents happen descending tired. Down-climb fixed lines on rappels.

### Day 16–17+ — Descend, fly out
- High Camp → 14 Camp → 11 Camp → Base Camp in 2–3 days
- Wait for air taxi window — could be hours, could be 3+ days
- **Build buffer days** into your return flight booking

## Key gear (highlights — full list in [[Denali Gear List]])

This is where gear becomes life-and-death. Highlights only here:

- **8000 m double boots** (La Sportiva Olympus Mons Cube or Scarpa Phantom 8000). The single most expensive item; do not skimp.
- **–40 °F down parka** (Feathered Friends Volant or Rab Expedition)
- **–40 °F sleeping bag** (Feathered Friends Snowbunting EX 0 or Western Mountaineering Bison GWS)
- **Mountaineering tent rated for severe storms** (Hilleberg Keron 3, Black Diamond First Light, MSR Access 3)
- **Two stoves** (MSR XGK EX × 2, white gas, with spares)
- **Plastic sleds + harnesses** for hauling
- **Pickets, ice screws, fixed-line ascender** (Petzl Ascension or Wild Country Ropeman)
- **Pee bottle + CMC (Clean Mountain Can)** — required, picked up at ranger station

## Risks & decisions specific to Denali

> [!warning] Cold injury
> –40 °F at high camp, –20 °F is "warm." Most Denali injuries are **frostbite of fingers, toes, nose, cheeks**. Drill the buddy face check. Vapor-barrier liners in boots from 14k up.

> [!warning] HAPE / HACE
> 20,310 ft, no acclimatization plateau higher than 14 Camp before summit push. **Carry dex + nifedipine and know dosing.** Descend at first sign of HACE — no exceptions.

> [!warning] Crevasse falls
> The Kahiltna Glacier is heavily crevassed below 14,200 ft. **Rope-team protocol from the moment you step off the air taxi until you step back on.** Crevasse rescue drill (haul system) must be reflexive.

> [!warning] Storms at High Camp
> Multi-day storms at 17 Camp are routine. Snow walls, double-anchor your tent (deadman buried, picket above). Plan for **3+ days pinned**. Don't burn fuel on summit attempts in bad weather.

> [!warning] Descent tiredness
> ~70% of fatal accidents on Denali happen **during descent**. Pace yourself on summit day. Don't summit at 18:00.

## Solo vs partner vs guided

- **Solo:** legal, but NPS requires substantial demonstrated experience. Crevasse risk on Kahiltna is much higher solo. Not recommended for a first Denali.
- **Partner team of 2–4 (unguided):** the standard. 3 is the optimal team size — rope-of-three crevasse rescue, load-sharing, redundancy.
- **Guided:** AAI, Alpine Ascents, RMI, Mountain Trip — $11,500–14,500/person. Worth considering if no rope partner emerges or if your team lacks Alaska experience. See [[Guides & Operators]].

## Pre-trip checklist (year out)

**Year out**
- [ ] Lock the rope team (2–3 partners, share expectations + experience)
- [ ] Book Denali season (typically May 15 – June 30 sweet spot)
- [ ] Begin altitude prep: Cotopaxi, Chimborazo good; Aconcagua ideal if doable
- [ ] Begin training plan ([[Training Plan]])

**6 months out**
- [ ] Submit NPS registration packet (60-day minimum)
- [ ] Lock air taxi operator + dates
- [ ] Order specialty gear (8000m boots need months of break-in)
- [ ] Renew Global Rescue / AAC

**3 months out**
- [ ] Test entire system in cold weather (sleep out in –20 °F or colder)
- [ ] Crevasse rescue drill day with rope team
- [ ] Confirm food + fuel plan with team
- [ ] Sleds + harnesses dialed

**Trip start**
- [ ] Talkeetna check-in
- [ ] Watch NPS orientation video
- [ ] Get CMC, file itinerary
- [ ] Buy fuel (don't fly with it)
- [ ] Last calorie loading
